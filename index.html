Questions, answered.

What is an index perpetual?

A single derivative contract that tracks a basket of assets at fixed weights. Instead of opening five separate trades to express one view, you open one — with one margin, one exit, and one liquidation level covering the whole basket.

How are the weights decided?

Weights are set once, when the index is created, and don't rebalance on their own. If the mix stops matching your thesis, you close the position or move to a different index — the weights themselves never silently drift.

Where does the price come from?

Directly from the pool backing that index, priced against its constituent assets — there's no order book or market maker standing in between you and the basket.

Do I need a wallet to look around?

No. Markets, indices, and pool depth are all public and browsable without connecting anything. A wallet is only needed once you're ready to open a position.

Can anyone create an index?

Yes. Anyone can define a basket, set its fixed weights, and seed the pool that prices it — that pool's liquidity is what makes the index tradeable for everyone else.
